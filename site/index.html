How it works

Three things happen the moment you open a ticket. Nothing to set up, nothing to train.

1

It finds the same work

It reads your project's most recent finished tickets and picks the closest matches: same type, same component, similar words in the title.

2

It measures what really happened

How many working days those tickets took from start to done, and how often they met their own due date. If this ticket is already under way, only tickets that ran at least as long count.

3

It tells you, with proof

On track, tight or likely to slip, the date 7 in 10 similar tickets made, and the past tickets behind it, one click away. One button sets the realistic date, with Undo.

Questions

Does it use AI to guess dates?

No. The dates come from plain counting over your own finished tickets: how long similar work took and how often it met its due date. You can check every number by clicking the tickets behind it. Rovo is only used to let you ask in plain words; it repeats what the app worked out.

How much history does it need?

At least 3 finished tickets in the project, and it gets sharper with more. It reads the project's 500 most recent finished tickets, so it stays current as the team changes. With fewer than 5 close matches it widens to the same ticket type, then the whole project, and says so.

Does my data leave Jira?

No. Due Date Check runs on Atlassian's own Forge platform with no outside servers, and it reads tickets as the person viewing them, so nobody sees anything they couldn't already see. See Privacy.

Will it change my tickets?

Only when you press "Use the realistic date", confirm it, and even then you can Undo. The Rovo agent never changes tickets.

What counts as a working day?

Monday to Friday. Start is when the ticket was created; done is when it was resolved.

Which Jira does it work with?

Jira Cloud: business projects and software projects alike. It needs only the standard Due date field.

What does it cost?

It's free while in beta.
